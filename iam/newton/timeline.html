Isaac Newton

biographical · standard · model: claude-opus-4-8 · 2026-08-05
Birth and Early Life 1642 – 1661
1642 Dec 25
Born at Woolsthorpe Manor
Born prematurely in Lincolnshire (25 December Old Style; 4 January 1643 Gregorian). His father had died three months before his birth.
1646
Mother remarries; left with grandmother
His mother Hannah married Reverend Barnabas Smith and moved away, leaving young Isaac to be raised by his maternal grandmother.
c. 1655
The King's School, Grantham
Attended the grammar school at Grantham, lodging with the apothecary Mr. Clarke, where he showed early aptitude for mechanical models.
Cambridge and the Miracle Years 1661 – 1669
1661 Jun
Admitted to Trinity College, Cambridge
Entered as a subsizar, paying his way through service. He immersed himself in the works of Descartes, Galileo, and other modern thinkers.
1665
Earns Bachelor of Arts degree
Graduated from Trinity College shortly before the university closed due to the Great Plague.
1665 – 1666
Plague closure and the "anni mirabiles"
During the plague retreat at Woolsthorpe he developed early ideas on calculus, the theory of colors, and gravitation.
Professorship and Discovery 1669 – 1696
1669
Appointed Lucasian Professor of Mathematics
Succeeded Isaac Barrow to the prestigious Cambridge chair at the age of 26.
1672
Elected Fellow of the Royal Society
His reflecting telescope and a paper on light and colors brought him into the Society, sparking a long dispute with Robert Hooke.
1687 Jul 5
Publishes the Principia Mathematica
Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica, published with Edmond Halley's encouragement and funding, set out the laws of motion and universal gravitation.
1689
Member of Parliament for Cambridge
Represented the University of Cambridge in the Convention Parliament following the Glorious Revolution.
The Mint and Public Life 1696 – 1705
1696
Appointed Warden of the Mint
Moved to London and oversaw the Great Recoinage, vigorously pursuing counterfeiters and clippers.
1699
Promoted to Master of the Mint
Assumed the senior and lucrative office, which he held for the remainder of his life.
1703
Elected President of the Royal Society
Assumed leadership of the Society after Hooke's death, a post he retained until his own death.
1704
Publishes Opticks
Presented his experiments with prisms and the composition of white light, written largely in English rather than Latin.
1705 Apr 16
Knighted by Queen Anne
Knighted at Trinity College, Cambridge—likely for political reasons—becoming Sir Isaac Newton.
Disputes and Final Years 1710 – 1727
1712 – 1713
Calculus priority dispute with Leibniz
A Royal Society committee, guided by Newton, ruled in his favor over Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, defining a bitter late conflict.
1713
Second edition of the Principia
Published a revised edition edited by Roger Cotes, including the famous "General Scholium" with his theological reflections.
1727 Mar 20
Death in London
Died in his sleep (Old Style; 31 March Gregorian) at Kensington and was buried with honors in Westminster Abbey.
1728
Posthumous chronology published
The Chronology of Ancient Kingdoms Amended appeared after his death, reflecting his lifelong writings on history, theology, and prophecy.
